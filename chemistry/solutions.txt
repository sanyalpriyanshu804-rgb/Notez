1. SOLUTIONS & SOLUBILITY
• Solution: Homogeneous mixture (uniform composition throughout).
• Binary Solution: 2 components → Solute (less quantity) + Solvent (more quantity).
• Physical state of Solution = Physical state of Solvent.
  - Types: Solid, Liquid, Gaseous solutions.

• Solubility: Maximum amount of solute dissolved in a specified amount of solvent at fixed T & P.
• Principle: "Like dissolves like" → Polar solute in Polar solvent; Non-polar in Non-polar.
• Saturated Solution Equilibrium: Solute + Solvent ⇌ Solution
  - Forward = Dissolution; Backward = Crystallization. At equilibrium: Rate of dissolution = Rate of crystallization.

• Effect of Temperature on Solid in Liquid:    # eqn m heat dal do fir la chatelier
  - Endothermic (ΔH_sol > 0): T ↑ → Solubility ↑; T ↓ → Solubility ↓.
  - Exothermic (ΔH_sol < 0): T ↑ → Solubility ↓; T ↓ → Solubility ↑.

• Effect of Pressure on Solid in Liquid:
  - Solids and liquids are non-compressible → Pressure has NO effect on solubility of solids in liquids.

2. GAS SOLUBILITY IN LIQUID & HENRY'S LAW
• Dissolution of Gas in Liquid is Exothermic (ΔH < 0): T ↑ → Solubility ↓.
# global warming aquatic life k liye khatra
• Effect of Pressure: P ↑ → Volume of gas ↓ → Contact with liquid surface ↑ → Gas solubility ↑.

• ⭐ Henry's Law: Partial pressure of gas (p) ∝ Mole fraction of gas in solution (χ_gas).
  Formula: p = K_H × χ_gas
  - p = Partial pressure of gas
  - χ_gas = Mole fraction of gas in solution
  - K_H = Henry's constant (depends on nature of gas & temperature).

• K_H Relationships & Conditions:
  - At constant P: K_H ∝ 1 / χ_gas
  - K_H ↑ → Solubility in solution ↓
  - Graph: p vs χ_gas → Straight line through origin (y = mx, slope = K_H).

• Application: Scuba divers going deep → P ↑ → Gas solubility in blood ↑. Returning to surface → P ↓ rapidly → Gas comes out of blood forming bubbles → Causes "Bends" (painful/dangerous condition). # carbonated drinks # anoxia

• Limitations: Gases should not chemically react or associates or dissociate with solvent (HCl ✕)

3. RAOULT'S LAW & VAPOR PRESSURE
• Vapor Pressure (VP): Pressure exerted by vapors on liquid surface at dynamic equilibrium (Rate of vaporization = Rate of condensation).

• ⭐ Raoult's Law (Volatile liquids A and B):
For a solution of volatile liquids, the partial vapour pressure of each component of the solution is directly proportional to it's mole fraction present in solution.

  p_A = p°_A × χ_A
  p_B = p°_B × χ_B
  - p_A, p_B = Partial VP of A and B
  - p°_A, p°_B = VP of pure liquids A and B
  - χ_A, χ_B = Mole fraction of A and B in liquid phase (χ_A + χ_B = 1)
• ⭐ Total Vapor Pressure:
  P_total = p_A + p_B = p°_A(χ_A) + p°_B(χ_B)
  P_total = p°_A + (p°_B - p°_A) × χ_B # iska derivation h

• ⭐ Mole Fraction in Vapor Phase (y_A, y_B):
  y_A = p_A / P_total
  y_B = p_B / P_total
  - Note: χ_A, χ_B are in liquid phase; y_A, y_B are in vapor phase!

• Non-Volatile Solute (Solid B) in Volatile Solvent (A):
  - Solute B non-volatile → p°_B = 0.
  - Solute occupies surface area → Volatile solvent molecules on surface ↓ → Rate of vaporization ↓.
  - VP of Solution (P_S) < VP of Pure Solvent (p°_A).
  - P_S = p_A = p°_A × χ_A

4. IDEAL & NON-IDEAL SOLUTIONS
• Ideal Solutions:
  - Obeys Raoult's Law over entire concentration range.
  - P_obs = P_calc.
  - Intermolecular forces: e_AA ≈ e_BB ≈ e_AB.
  - ΔV_mix = 0 (10 mL + 10 mL = 20 mL).
  - ΔH_mix = 0 (No heat released or absorbed).
  - ΔG_mix < 0 (Spontaneous process).
  - ΔS_mix > 0 (Entropy/randomness increases).
  - Examples (Same family): CH₄ + C₂H₆, Chlorobenzene + Bromobenzene.

• Non-Ideal Solutions (P_obs ≠ P_calc):
  1. Positive Deviation:
     - P_obs > P_calc
     - Forces: e_AB < e_AA, e_BB (Interactions weaken → Vapors escape easily)
     - ΔV_mix > 0 (10 mL + 10 mL > 20 mL)
     - ΔH_mix > 0 (Endothermic process)
     - ΔG_mix < 0, ΔS_mix > 0
     - Examples:"Water + Alcohol", CCl₄ + CHCl₃, "Acetone + CS₂", "ethanol + acetone"

  2. Negative Deviation:
     - P_obs < P_calc
     - Forces: e_AB > e_AA, e_BB (Interactions strengthen → Vapors form with difficulty)
     - ΔV_mix < 0 (10 mL + 10 mL < 20 mL)
     - ΔH_mix < 0 (Exothermic process)
     - ΔG_mix < 0, ΔS_mix > 0
     - Examples: Water + Inorganic Acid (e.g., Water + HCl), "Phenol + Aniline"

5. AZEOTROPIC MIXTURES
• Azeotrope: Binary liquid mixture boiling at constant temperature 'at a unique composition' with same composition in liquid and vapor phase. Cannot be separated by fractional distillation. This phenomena is known as azeotropy.
• Formed only by Non-Ideal solutions at specific composition.

• Types:
  1. Minimum Boiling Azeotrope:
     - Formed by solutions showing large Positive Deviation.
     - B.P. of azeotrope < B.P. of either component (VP ↑ → B.P. ↓).
     - Ex. "alcohol + water"
  2. Maximum Boiling Azeotrope:
     - Formed by solutions showing large Negative Deviation.
     - B.P. of azeotrope > B.P. of either component (VP ↓ → B.P. ↑).
     - Ex. "HNO3 + H20", H2O + HCl

6. COLLIGATIVE PROPERTIES
• Definition: Properties depending ONLY on number of solute particles, NOT on their nature.
• Conditions: Solution must be dilute + Solute must be non-volatile solid.

A. Relative Lowering of Vapor Pressure (RLVP):
• Lowering in VP = p°_A - P_S
• ⭐ RLVP Formula:
  (p°_A - P_S) / p°_A = χ_B = n_B / (n_A + n_B) # iska derivation h
• For dilute solution (n_A >> n_B):
  (p°_A - P_S) / p°_A = n_B / n_A = (w_B × M_A) / (M_B × w_A)
• Alternate useful form:    # only shortcut
  (p°_A - P_S) / P_S = n_B / n_A = (w_B × M_A) / (M_B × w_A)

B. Elevation of Boiling Point (ΔT_b):
• Boiling Point: Temperature where VP of liquid = External pressure (1 atm).
• B.P. of Solution (T_b) > B.P. of Pure Solvent (T°_b).
• Elevation: ΔT_b = T_b - T°_b
• ⭐ Formula:
  ΔT_b = K_b × m # iska derivation h
  ΔT_b = K_b × [(w_B × 1000) / (M_B × w_A)]
  - m = Molality, w_B = Solute mass (g), w_A = Solvent mass (g), M_B = Solute molar mass.
  - K_b = Molal elevation constant / Ebullioscopic constant (depends ONLY on solvent; Water K_b = 0.52 K kg mol⁻¹).
• K_b Relation: K_b = (M_A × R × (T°_b)²) / (1000 × ΔH_vap)    # not in boards

C. Depression of Freezing Point (ΔT_f):
• Freezing Point: Temperature where VP of liquid phase = VP of solid phase.
• F.P. of Pure Solvent (T°_f) > F.P. of Solution (T_f).
• Depression: ΔT_f = T°_f - T_f
• ⭐ Formula:
  ΔT_f = K_f × m # iska derivation h
  ΔT_f = K_f × [(w_B × 1000) / (M_B × w_A)]
  - K_f = Molal depression constant / Cryoscopic constant ( depends only on nature of solvent, Water K_f = 1.86 K kg mol⁻¹).
• K_f Relation: K_f = (M_A × R × (T°_f)²) / (1000 × ΔH_fusion)    # not in boards

D. Osmotic Pressure (π):
• Osmosis: Movement of solvent particles from lower concentration (pure solvent) to higher concentration (solution) through Semi-Permeable Membrane (SPM).
• Osmotic Pressure (π): Excess pressure applied on solution side to just stop osmosis.
• ⭐ Van't Hoff Formula:
  π = C × R × T
  - C = Molarity (mol/L) = n_B / V
  - R = 0.0821 L atm K⁻¹ mol⁻¹
  - T = Temperature in Kelvin (K)
• Solution Types:
  - Isotonic: π₁ = π₂ → C₁ = C₂ (at same T)
  - Hypertonic: Higher π (Higher C)
  - Hypotonic: Lower π (Lower C)
  - Osmosis Flow: Hypotonic → Hypertonic (Low π → High π).
• Resulting Osmotic Pressure on Mixing: π_R = (π₁V₁ + π₂V₂) / (V₁ + V₂)

• Reverse Osmosis (RO): Applied Pressure > π → Solvent flows from Solution to Pure Solvent side. Used in water purification/desalination.
• Note: Best colligative property for determining molar mass of macromolecules, polymers, biomolecules.

7. ABNORMAL MOLAR MASS & VAN'T HOFF FACTOR (i)
• Cause: Association or Dissociation of solute in solution.
  - Dissociation: Particle count ↑ → Colligative Property ↑ → Observed Molar Mass ↓.
  - Association: Particle count ↓ → Colligative Property ↓ → Observed Molar Mass ↑.
  - Example: Acetic acid in Benzene forms dimer ((CH₃COOH)₂) → Normal M_B = 60, Observed M_B = 120.

• ⭐ Van't Hoff Factor (i):
  i = Observed Colligative Property / Calculated Colligative Property
  i = Normal (Calculated) Molar Mass / Abnormal (Observed) Molar Mass
  i = Number of particles after dissociation/association / Initial number of particles

• ⭐ Formulas for i:
  - Dissociation: i = 1 - α + n α = 1 + (n - 1)α # iska derivation h
    (α = degree of ionization, n = number of ions; i > 1; Complete dissociation α = 1 ⇒ i = n)
    Examples: NaCl (n=2), CaCl₂ (n=3), K₄[Fe(CN)₆] (n=5).
  - Association: i = 1 - α + (α / n) # iska derivation h
    (α = degree of association, n = number of associating molecules; i < 1; Dimer n=2, Trimer n=3)

  - Non-electrolytes: Glucose, Urea, Sucrose → i = 1, α = 0.

• ⭐ UNIVERSAL COLLIGATIVE PROPERTY FORMULAS (WITH i):
  1. RLVP: (p°_A - P_S) / p°_A = (i × n_B) / (i × n_B + n_A)
  2. Elevation of B.P.: ΔT_b = i × K_b × m
  3. Depression of F.P.: ΔT_f = i × K_f × m
  4. Osmotic Pressure: π = i × C × R × T

When 25.6 g of Sulphur was dissolved in 1000 g of benzene, the freezing point lowered by 0.512 K . Calculate the formula of Sulphur (Sx).
(Kf for benzene =5.12 K kg mol−1, Atomic mass of Sulphur =32 gmol−1 )
- Given:
 Mass of sulphur = 25.6 g
 Mass of benzene = 1000 g = 1 kg
 Depression in freezing point (ΔTf) = 0.512 K
 Kf of benzene = 5.12 K kg mol^-1
 Atomic mass of sulphur = 32 g mol^-1

 Formula:
 ΔTf = Kf × m

 Therefore:
 m = ΔTf / Kf
 m = 0.512 / 5.12
 m = 0.1 mol kg^-1

 Since benzene = 1 kg:
 Moles of sulphur = 0.1 mol

 Molar mass of sulphur:
 M = Mass / Moles
 M = 25.6 / 0.1
 M = 256 g mol^-1

 Let the formula of sulphur be Sx.
 Then:
 32x = 256
 x = 256 / 32
 x = 8
 Therefore, the formula of sulphur is S8.
