1. ADDITION COMPOUNDS: DOUBLE SALT VS COORDINATION COMPLEX
• Double Salt: Stable in solid state, but dissociates completely (100%) into simple constituent ions in aqueous solution.
  - Examples: Carnallite (KCl·MgCl₂·6H₂O), Potash Alum (K₂SO₄·Al₂(SO₄)₃·24H₂O).
  - Gives positive tests for all constituent ions; loses identity in solution.
• Coordination Complex: Retains its identity in aqueous solution; does not dissociate completely into simple ions.
  - Example: K₄[Fe(CN)₆] → 4K⁺ + [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻ (Does NOT give tests for free Fe²⁺ or CN⁻ ions).

2. TERMINOLOGY
• Central Metal (CM): Metal atom/ion bound to fixed number of ligands in a definite geometry.
• Ligand: Neutral molecule or ion bound to CM by donating electron pair(s) via coordinate bond(s).
• Coordination number: The number of coordination bonds formed by the CM with ligangs.
• Homolyptic and Heterolyptic complexes: Complexes in which CM is bonded to only one kind of ligands, are known as homoleptic otherwise heterolyptic.

CLASSIFICATION OF LIGANDS
• Charge-Based Classification:
  - Anionic (-o ending): Cl⁻ (chlorido/chloro), CN⁻ (cyano), C₂O₄²⁻ (oxalato), OH⁻ (hydroxo), O²⁻ (oxo).
  - Neutral: H₂O (aqua), NH₃ (ammine), CO (carbonyl), en (ethane-1,2-diamine), py (pyridine), PPh₃ (triphenylphosphine).
  - Cationic (-ium ending): NO⁺ (nitrosonium), NO₂⁺ (nitronium), NH₂NH₃⁺ (hydrazinium).

• Denticity: Number of coordinate bonds formed by a single ligand with the CM.
  - Monodentate: Forms 1 coordinate bond (e.g., H₂O, NH₃, Cl⁻, CN⁻).
  - Bidentate: Forms 2 coordinate bonds simultaneously via 2 donor atoms.
    • Symmetrical: en (ethane-1,2-diamine), ox²⁻ (oxalato), bpy (bipyridine).
    • Unsymmetrical: gly⁻ (glycinato), dmg⁻ (dimethylglyoximato).
  - Polydentate: Forms >2 coordinate bonds.
    • EDTA⁴⁻ (ethylenediaminetetraacetate): Hexadentate (2 N + 4 O donors).
    • EDTA³⁻: Pentadentate (2 N + 3 O donors).

• Ambidentate Ligand: Monodentate ligand possessing >1 potential donor atom, but coordinates through ONLY ONE atom at a time.
  - Examples: CN⁻ / NC⁻ (cyano-C / cyano-N), NO₂⁻ / ONO⁻ (nitrite-N / nitrite-O), SCN⁻ / NCS⁻ (thiocyanate-S / thiocyanate-N).

• Flexidentate Ligand: Ligand that can vary its denticity based on the requirement of CM (e.g., EDTA⁴⁻, SO₄²⁻, CO₃²⁻).

• Chelating Ligand: Multidentate ligand that binds to CM forming a cyclic ring structure (chelate ring).
  - Chelate Effect: Complexes with chelate rings are significantly MORE stable than non-chelated complexes.
  - Note: 3-membered rings are unstable due to strain; 5- and 6-membered rings are most stable.

3. IUPAC NOMENCLATURE RULES
• Cation is named first, followed by anion.
• Inside Coordination Sphere: Name ligands alphabetically first, then name the Central Metal.
• Numerical Prefixes: Use di, tri, tetra for simple ligands. Use bis, tris, tetrakis for complex ligands whose name already contains di, tri, etc. (e.g., tris(ethane-1,2-diamine)).
• Oxidation State of CM is written in Roman numerals inside parentheses ( ).
• Anionic Complex Entity: Central metal name MUST end with suffix "-ate".
  - Examples: Ferrate (Fe), Cuprate (Cu), Argentate (Ag), Zincate (Zn), Chromate (Cr), Plumbate (Pb), Cobaltate (Co), Aluminat (Al), Stannate (Sn), Nickelate (Ni), Mercurate (Hg).
• Cationic / Neutral Complex Entity: Central metal retains its standard elemental name.
• Water of Crystallization: Named as "hydrate" (e.g., .2H₂O → dihydrate).
• Complex Ion with Charge: Append the word "ion" at the end.

4. WERNER'S THEORY
• Primary Valency (PV):
  - Corresponding to Oxidation State (OS) of central metal.
  - Ionizable, non-directional.
  - Satisfied ONLY by negative ions. Represented by dotted lines (...).
• Secondary Valency (SV):
  - Corresponding to Coordination Number (CN) of central metal.
  - Non-ionizable, directional (determines complex geometry).
  - Satisfied by neutral molecules or negative ions. Fixed for a metal. Represented by solid lines (—).
• Solution Behavior & Conductance:
  - Electrical Conductance ∝ Total number of ions produced in solution ∝ Charge on ions.
  - Colligative Properties (ΔT_b, ΔT_f, π) ∝ Total number of solute particles/ions.

5. EFFECTIVE ATOMIC NUMBER (EAN)
• EAN: Total number of electrons present around the central metal atom/ion in a complex.
• ⭐ Formula: EAN = Z - (ON) + 2 × (CN)
  - Z = Atomic number of Central Metal
  - ON = Oxidation State of Central Metal
  - CN = Coordination Number of Central Metal

6. VALENCE BOND THEORY (VBT)
• Coordination Number & Hybridization:
  - CN = 2 → sp (Linear)
  - CN = 4 → sp³ (Tetrahedral) OR dsp² (Square Planar, uses d_x²-y²)
  - CN = 6 → d²sp³ (Inner Orbital / Low Spin Complex) OR sp³d² (Outer Orbital / High Spin Complex)
    • Inner/Outer d-orbitals used for Octahedral: d_x²-y² and d_z².

• Strong Field Ligand (SFL): Forces pairing of d-electrons against Hund's rule (e.g., CN⁻, CO, NH₃).
• Weak Field Ligand (WFL): Cannot force pairing of d-electrons (e.g., F⁻, Cl⁻, Br⁻, I⁻, H₂O).

• Magnetic Properties:
  - Paramagnetic: At least 1 unpaired electron (n ≥ 1).
  - Diamagnetic: Zero unpaired electrons (n = 0).
• ⭐ Spin-Only Magnetic Moment (μ):
  μ = √(n(n + 2)) BM
  - n = number of unpaired electrons; Unit = Bohr Magneton (BM).

• VBT Limitations: Cannot explain spectrochemical series, quantitative color/absorption spectra, kinetic stability, or why certain CN=4 complexes are tetrahedral vs square planar.

7. CRYSTAL FIELD THEORY (CFT)
• Electrostatic Model: Considers metal-ligand bond as purely ionic, treating CM as positive point charge and ligands as negative point charges/dipoles.
• d-Orbital Division:
  - Non-Axial Set (t₂g): d_xy, d_yz, d_zx (lobes lie between coordinate axes).
  - Axial Set (e_g): d_x²-y², d_z² (lobes lie directly along coordinate axes).

• ⭐ Octahedral Splitting (Δ_o):
  - Ligands approach along x, y, z axes.
  - Axial e_g orbitals experience higher repulsion → Energy increases by +0.6 Δ_o (+3/5 Δ_o).
  - Non-axial t₂g orbitals experience lesser repulsion → Energy decreases by -0.4 Δ_o (-2/5 Δ_o).
  - Energy gap between t₂g and e_g = Δ_o (Crystal Field Splitting Energy).

• ⭐ Tetrahedral Splitting (Δ_t):
  - Ligands approach between axes.
  - t₂g orbitals experience higher repulsion → Energy increases by +0.4 Δ_t (+2/5 Δ_t).
  - e_g orbitals experience lesser repulsion → Energy decreases by -0.6 Δ_t (-3/5 Δ_t).
  - Relationship with Octahedral: Δ_t = (4/9) × Δ_o

• Splitting Magnitude Order: Δ_sq > Δ_o > Δ_t (Δ_sq ≈ 1.3 Δ_o).

• ⭐ Spectrochemical Series (Ligand Field Strength ↑):
  I⁻ < Br⁻ < SCN⁻ < Cl⁻ < S²⁻ < F⁻ < OH⁻ < C₂O₄²⁻ < H₂O < NCS⁻ < EDTA⁴⁻ < NH₃ < en < CN⁻ < CO
  - WFL (H₂O and below): Δ_o < P (Pairing Energy) → No electron pairing → High Spin Complex.
  - SFL (NH₃ and above): Δ_o > P → Electron pairing occurs in t₂g → Low Spin Complex.

8. COLOR OF COORDINATION COMPOUNDS
• Cause: d-d transition of electron from lower energy set (t₂g) to higher energy set (e_g) upon absorbing specific wavelength (λ) of visible light.
• Observed Color = Complementary color of light absorbed (determined via Color Wheel).
• Conditions for Color:
  1. Must have d¹ to d⁹ electron configuration (d⁰ and d¹⁰ are colorless as d-d transition is impossible).
  2. Presence of ligand field / splitting is mandatory (No ligands = No splitting = Colorless).
  - Example: CuSO₄·5H₂O is blue (water ligands cause splitting); Anhydrous CuSO₄ is white/colorless. Heating CuSO₄·5H₂O drives off H₂O → becomes colorless.

9. STABILITY OF COMPLEXES
• Overall Formation / Stability Constant (K): M + 4L ⇌ ML₄
  K = [ML₄] / ([M][L]⁴)
• Stepwise Stability Constants: K = k₁ × k₂ × k₃ × k₄ # iska derivation h
• Instability / Dissociation Constant = 1 / K
• Factors Increasing Complex Stability:
  - Central Metal Charge / OS ↑ → Stability ↑
  - Central Metal Size ↓ → Stability ↑ (Z_eff ↑ → Stability ↑)
  - Ligand Field Strength ↑ (SFL > WFL) → Stability ↑
  - Chelate Effect: Number of chelate rings ↑ → Stability ↑

10. SYNERGIC BONDING IN METAL CARBONYLS
• Dual Bonding Mechanism:
  1. M ← C σ-bond: Lone pair donation from filled C-orbital of CO into vacant d-orbital of CM.
  2. M → C π-bond (Back Bonding): Donation from filled d-orbital of CM into vacant π* (anti-bonding) orbital of CO.
• Synergic Effect:
  - Strengthens M-C bond → M-C bond order ↑ → M-C bond length ↓.
  - Weakens C-O bond → C-O bond order ↓ → C-O bond length ↑.
  - Extent of Synergic Bonding ∝ Negative oxidation state on CM (More negative ON on metal = Stronger back bonding).

11. ISOMERISM IN COORDINATION COMPOUNDS
• A. Structural Isomerism:
  1. Ionization Isomerism: Swapping of counter ion and ligand in sphere → Yields different ions in aq. solution.
  2. Linkage Isomerism: Occurs when ambidentate ligand is present in complex (e.g., -NO₂ vs -ONO).
  3. Solvate / Hydrate Isomerism: Different distribution of H₂O molecules inside vs outside coordination sphere.
  4. Coordination Isomerism: Exchange of ligands between cationic and anionic complex entities.

• B. Stereoisomerism:
  1. Geometrical Isomerism (GI):
     - Square Planar [CN=4]: [MA₄], [MA₃B] → No GI; [MA₂B₂] → 2 GI (Cis/Trans); [MABCD] → 3 GI.
     - Octahedral [CN=6]: [MA₆], [MA₅B] → No GI; [MA₄B₂] → 2 GI (Cis/Trans); [MA₃B₃] → 2 GI (Facial/fac & Meridional/mer).
  2. Optical Isomerism (OI):
     - Requires chiral molecule (Absence of Plane of Symmetry / POS).
     - Square Planar: Generally optically inactive due to molecular POS.
     - Octahedral: [M(AA)₃] → Optically active (d and l enantiomers); Cis-[M(AA)₂B₂] → Optically active; Trans-[M(AA)₂B₂] → Optically inactive (POS present).
