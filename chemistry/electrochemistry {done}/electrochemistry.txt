1. FUNDAMENTALS AND CELL TYPES
- Electrochemistry: Study of interconversion of chemical and electrical energy.
- Galvanic/Voltaic/Electrochemical Cell: Chemical → Electrical; Spontaneous (ΔG = -ve); Anode(-ve), Cathode(+ve).
- Electrolytic Cell: Electrical → Chemical; Non-spontaneous (ΔG = +ve); Anode(+ve), Cathode(-ve).
- 📌 Anode: Always Oxidation; Cathode: Always Reduction.
- Reversibility: stops if opposing EMF is equal; reverses if opposing EMF > cell EMF.
- Electrode Potential: Arises from charge separation at rod-solution interface due to de-electronation (M → Mⁿ⁺ + ne⁻) or electronation (Mⁿ⁺ + ne⁻ → M).
- 📌 Direction of flow: Electrons (Anode → Cathode); Current (Cathode → Anode).

2. GALVANIC CELLS AND SALT BRIDGE
- ⭐ EMF Formula: E°cell = E°SRP(Cathode) - E°SRP(Anode).
- Daniell Cell: Zn(s) | Zn²⁺(aq) || Cu²⁺(aq) | Cu(s). E° = 1.1V.
- Salt Bridge Functions: Completes circuit; maintains electrical neutrality; minimizes Liquid-Liquid Junction Potential (LLJP).
- 📌 Electrolyte Criteria: Equal ionic mobility (K⁺ ≈ Cl⁻); chemically inert.
- ⚠️ "Choking" Trap: Do NOT use KCl if cell has Ag⁺, Pb²⁺, Hg₂²⁺, or Tl⁺; forms precipitate choking the bridge. Use NH₄NO₃ or KNO₃ instead.
- IUPAC Representation: Anode(Left) || Cathode(Right). Phase boundary (|), Salt Bridge (||).
- 📌 Gas Electrode: Pt | Gas | Ion (Anode); Ion | Gas | Pt (Cathode). Gas always next to Pt.

3. STANDARD HYDROGEN ELECTRODE (SHE) AND ECS
- SHE: Primary reference electrode; E° assigned 0.0V at all temperatures.
- Construction: Pt wire, H₂ gas (1 bar), 1M H⁺ ions (HCl).
- Standard Conditions: 1M concentration, 1 bar pressure, 298K.

- Electrochemical Series (ECS): Arrangement by increasing Standard Reduction Potential (SRP).
- 💡 Interpretation:
  - Higher SRP = Stronger Oxidizing Agent (F₂ has highest SRP = 2.87V).
  - Lower SRP = Stronger Reducing Agent (Li has lowest SRP = -3.05V).
- Metal Displacement: Metal with lower SRP displaces metal with higher SRP from salt solution.
- Reactivity with Acids: Metals with negative SRP liberate H₂ from dilute acids.
- ⭐ SRP = -SOP (Standard Oxidation Potential).

4. THERMODYNAMICS AND EMF
- ⭐ Free Energy: ΔG = -nFEcell; ΔG° = -nFE°cell.
- Constants: F (Faraday) = 96500 C/mol; n = moles of e⁻ canceled in net reaction.
- Spontaneity: Spontaneous if Ecell = +ve and ΔG = -ve.
- ⚠️ Intensive Property Trap: E° is intensive (not additive). ΔG is extensive (additive). To find E° for unknown half-reaction, combine ΔG° values, not E°.

5. NERNST EQUATION
- Purpose: Calculates actual EMF (E) when conditions ≠ standard (1M, 1 bar).
- ⭐ Master Formula (298K):
            Ecell = E°cell - (RT/nF) logₑ Q.
                Ecell = E°cell - (0.0591/n) log₁₀ Q.     # at 25ᵒC
- Q (Reaction Quotient): [Products] / [Reactants] raised to stoichiometry. 📌 Ignore solids/pure liquids.
- Hydrogen Electrode Potential: ⭐ E(H⁺/H₂) = -0.0591 × pH.
- Graphs: Ecell vs log₁₀ Q is linear with Slope = -0.0591/n and Intercept = E°cell.
- ⚠️ Concentration Cell: E°cell = 0 (electrodes same). Driven by log(Canode/Ccathode).
- 💡 Advanced Link: Use Ksp to find ion concentrations for Q (Ksp = [Mⁿ⁺][Xⁿ⁻]).
- Temperature Coefficient: ΔS = nF(dE/dT)p. Enthalpy: ΔH = -nFE + nFT(dE/dT)p.
- Equilibrium: ⭐ E°cell = (0.0591/n) log₁₀ Keq at 298K.

6. ELECTROLYSIS AND FARADAY'S LAWS
- Electrolysis - The process of decomposition of an electrolyte on passing electricity or electrical current through it's aq solution or molten state. The cell used for this process is called an electrolytic cell.
# in an electrolytic cell anode is (+ve) and cathode is (-ve)

- Faraday’s 1st Law: The mass (W) of any substance deposited or liberated at any
electrode is directly proportional to the quantity of electricity (Q) passed
through the electrolyte.
        ⭐ W = EIt / 96500 = ZIt.    # E = Molar mass / n-factor
- Faraday's 2nd Law: When the same quantity of electricity is passed through different electrolytes connected in series, the masses of the substances produced are proportional to their equivalent weights.
        W₁/E₁ = W₂/E₂ for cells in series. Equivalents deposited are equal.
- ⭐ Charge: Q = (n × n_f)F where n = moles, n_f = n-factor (valency/e⁻ transfer).
- % Efficiency = (W_actual / W_theoretical) × 100.
- 📌 Cathode Equivalents = Anode Equivalents.
- 💡 Gas at STP: 1F produces 11.2 L H₂ or 5.6 L O₂.

- Preferential Discharge Rules:
{ If more than one type of ion is attracted towards a particular electrode, then the ion is discharged which have lower discharge potential. }
  - Cathode: Higher SRP reduces first. Group 1/2 metals < H₂O; H₂ gas produced from water.
# CHAPA - Cu²⁺ Hg²⁺ Ag²⁺ Pt³⁺ Au³⁺, reduces before H₂O or H⁺
  - Anode: Higher SOP oxidizes first. Halides (Cl⁻, Br⁻, I⁻) > H₂O.
  - ⚠️ Overvoltage Trap: Cl₂ evolved instead of O₂ in aq. NaCl due to slow O₂ kinetics.
  - H₂SO₄: Dilute → O₂; Concentrated → S₂O₈²⁻ (peroxodisulphuric acid).

- Conductance G = (1/R)
- Resistance: R = ρ(l/A).
- Conductivity (κ): Reciprocal of resistivity (ρ).
                κ = G × (l/A).
- Cell Constant (G*): l/A. ⭐ κ = G × G* = G* / R.
- Molar Conductivity: ⭐ Λm = κ(1000) / Molarity.
        - Unit : Scm² mol⁻¹
- Eq. Conductivity: Λeq = κ(1000) / Normality.
        - Unit : Scm² eq⁻¹
- ⭐ Relation: Λm = Λeq × n-factor.
- Dilution Effects:
          - κ decreases (ions/mL ↓).
          - G, Λm, Λeq increase (volume effect/dissociation ↑).
- Strong Electrolytes: Λm = Λ°m - b√C (Debye-Hückel-Onsager).    # strong aur weak ka graph
- Kohlrausch's Law: ⭐ Λ°m = xλ°(+) + yλ°(-) at infinite dilution.
- 💡 Degree of Dissociation (α): α = Λm / Λ°m. Dissociation Constant: Ka = Cα² / (1-α).
- Mobility: H⁺ > OH⁻ > other ions (Grotthuss/jump mechanism).
- 💡 Hydration: Smaller ions (Li⁺) have larger hydrated radii and lower conductance than K⁺.

8. BATTERIES AND CORROSION
- Primary (Non-rechargeable):
  - Dry Cell: 1.5V. Zn anode, MnO₂ cathode. Zn²⁺ complexed by NH₃ to [Zn(NH₃)₄]²⁺.
  - Mercury Cell: 1.35V. Constant voltage as net reaction has no ions in solution.
- Secondary (Rechargeable):
  - Lead Storage: 2.0V/cell. Pb anode, PbO₂ cathode, 38% H₂SO₄. Charging restores density.
  - Ni-Cd Cell: 1.2V. Longer life, Cd anode, NiO(OH) cathode.
- Fuel Cells: H₂-O₂ cell, 70% efficient. Used in Apollo mission. Product = pure water.
- Corrosion: Fe → Fe²⁺ (Anode); O₂ → H₂O (Cathode). Rust = Fe₂O₃.xH₂O.
- Prevention: Galvanization (Zn coating - sacrificial); painting; cathodic protection (Mg/Zn).

Assertion (A) : For measuring resistance of an ionic solution an AC source is used.
Reason (R) : Concentration of ionic solution will change if DC source is used.
--- Ans ---
Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
    Assertion (A) is true: AC is used to measure the resistance/conductance of an ionic solution.
    Reason (R) is true: With DC, ions continuously migrate toward the electrodes, causing electrolysis and changing the concentration of the solution near the electrodes.
    Using AC rapidly reverses the direction of ion movement, minimizing net ion migration and concentration changes.
